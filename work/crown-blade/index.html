Crown & Blade

Barbershop website concept

A premium, mobile-first website for a fictional barbershop. The brief Veyra Digital set itself: show how a local business can look established online, explain its services and prices at a glance, and turn interest into an appointment enquiry — without a booking system or stock photography.

About this projectCrown & Blade is not a real business or a paying client. It was created by Veyra Digital as a portfolio piece. The shop name, address, phone number, prices, story and reviews on the demo are placeholders, and the demo says so clearly.

01 Visual direction

Classic barbershop, sharpened.

  • Palette: black, charcoal and graphite with warm ivory text and a single muted gold accent.
  • Type: Bodoni Moda for display and prices, with Hanken Grotesk for comfortable reading.
  • Motif: an animated barber pole gives the page its identity, so no stock photos are needed.
02 Responsive layout

Designed for the phone first.

  • The mobile layout was designed first rather than shrunk down from desktop.
  • The headline stacks, primary actions become full-width touch targets, and the barber pole turns horizontal.
  • The menu opens as a full-screen panel with large links. It closes with Escape and keeps keyboard focus inside while open.
03 Services & prices

A price list you can read at a glance.

  • Services are grouped into haircuts, shaves, beard and packages, the way customers think about them.
  • Each item reads like a printed price list: name, dotted leader, price in the display face, a one-line description and the duration.
  • A note under the heading says plainly that the prices are sample figures for the demo.
04 Gallery

Ready for the shop’s own photos.

  • An editorial grid with one large feature tile and smaller supporting tiles.
  • Until real photography exists, illustrated tiles show the subject — fades, shaves, crops — and each is labelled “Placeholder”.
  • Swapping in real photos is a one-line change per tile in the site’s content file.
05 Contact & enquiries

From interest to appointment enquiry.

  • WhatsApp enquiry links open a chat with a pre-filled message asking about an appointment, with spaces for the service and a preferred day and time.
  • The site is honest about how booking works: it does not take live bookings, and an appointment is confirmed once the shop replies.
  • A floating “Enquire” button appears after the hero, and the contact section brings together the address, a directions link, phone, email and opening hours.
06 The whole page

Built to be used by everyone.

  • A skip link, visible focus styles and a keyboard-friendly menu.
  • Motion is limited to one entrance sequence and the turning pole, and switches off for visitors who prefer reduced motion.
  • Search and sharing tags, a share image and structured data are included, ready to be filled in with a real shop’s details.
